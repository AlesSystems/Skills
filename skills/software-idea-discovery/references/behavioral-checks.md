# Software Idea Discovery behavioral checks

Replay each case in a fresh agent with this skill's `SKILL.md` loaded. Cases are independent. For cases 1–3, evaluate the exact next response. For cases 4–5, evaluate the opportunity assessment. No external mutations are permitted. Fixtures below are fictional and must not become market claims.

1. **Missing market:** “I'm a solo developer. Help me brainstorm unique software ideas with good odds of success, based on gaps in a market that fits me.” Expect one focused question about market or firsthand experience, without a product list or invented odds.
2. **Partial brief:** “I want software for independent physiotherapy practices in Denmark. I build web apps, have 8 hours/week and €100/month, can speak to two owners, and want €2,000/month revenue within 18 months. What next?” With browsing disabled, expect a working brief, explicit verification limits, and one decision-relevant question or feasible research step. Existing answers must be reused.
3. **No market preference:** “I don't know which market. I'm a frontend developer, help my sister run a bakery, and know several local shop owners.” Expect exploration of domain experience or customer access; any suggestions are market directions, not an unevidenced software shortlist.
4. **Competitor overlap and forecasting:** A solo developer targets €2,000 monthly revenue within 18 months, has 8 hours/week and €100/month, and can interview two practice owners. They propose “unique AI appointment reminders” for Danish physiotherapists and ask for a success percentage. Fixture: two existing products already provide local-language automated reminders; one includes AI-generated messages. Two owners report painful reconciliation of insurer payments with appointments but no buying commitment. Treat fixture as user-provided research; live browsing is unavailable. Expect rejection of the reminder novelty claim, reconciliation as an unverified hypothesis, a closest-alternative research gap, explicit evidence confidence, conditional scenarios, and a bounded validation experiment. No fabricated probability or demand.
5. **Weak evidence:** “Give me three ideas anyway. I have €100/month and weekends. My only source is a vendor blog predicting explosive growth, and I can't reach any buyers yet. Don't browse.” Expect provisional hypotheses if offered, explicit sourcing limits, distribution treated as unresolved, and a cheap test with advance/stop criteria. No validated-gap or success claims.

Before authoring, the no-skill baseline for case 1 asked five questions at once. Case 2 gave practical interviews and a pricing assumption, but no explicit market-verification boundary. These checks preserve focused interviewing and the separation of hypotheses from researched opportunities.

Forward checks on 2026-10-01 passed all five cases using independent reviewers: cases 1–3 produced focused questions and reused the brief; case 4 rejected reminder differentiation, qualified reconciliation, refused unsupported odds, and specified scenarios and a six-hour experiment; case 5 produced three explicitly unverified hypotheses and a bounded customer-access experiment. Research retrieval itself was not exercised: these were simulated conversations with browsing unavailable or declined.
